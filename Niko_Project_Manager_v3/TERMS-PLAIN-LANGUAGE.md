# Niko Residential Holdings — Plain-Language Terms Guide

This guide explains what the standard terms are intended to mean. The signed project documents control. It is not legal advice and should be reviewed by an Ohio attorney.

## Contracting company

The customer contracts with **Amr and Nhi LLC**, operating as **Niko Residential Holdings**. A representative signs for the LLC, not as an individual guarantor, unless a separate written personal guarantee is deliberately signed.

## Written scope and changes

The company promises only the work written in the accepted quote, customer contract, approved change orders, and attachments. Added work or hidden conditions can change price and schedule only through proper written approval, except emergency protective steps allowed by law.

## Hidden conditions

The initial price does not automatically cover concealed mold, rot, pests, water damage, unsafe wiring, structural movement, code problems, hazardous material, or defective work hidden behind existing finishes.

## Materials

The customer pays material costs unless the project document says otherwise. Customer-supplied products must be suitable and available on time. Manufacturer defects and product warranties are generally handled through the manufacturer.

## In-house work and freelancers

The company may use Amr, an in-house crew, vendors, or qualified freelancers. The customer document identifies the work arrangement, but private company-to-freelancer compensation stays in the internal freelancer agreement.

The customer sees the agreed customer price. The freelancer sees only the compensation Amr and Nhi LLC agrees to pay the freelancer. The freelancer document does not display the company’s customer price or margin.

## Access and property protection

The customer provides safe access, working utilities when needed, parking instructions, and information about alarms, cameras, utilities, and hazards. Children, pets, valuables, vehicles, and fragile property should stay away from the work area.

## Cleanup and trash

Unless the written scope promises more, the immediate work area is left broom-clean. Dumpsters, hauling, deep cleaning, hazardous-material handling, and removal of old or unrelated trash require written inclusion and pricing.

## Existing damage and liability

Before-work photographs document visible conditions. The company is not automatically responsible for unrelated, concealed, or pre-existing damage. The agreement does not excuse negligence, willful misconduct, or responsibility that Ohio law does not permit a business to limit.

## 60-day workmanship warranty

The warranty covers qualifying defects caused by company workmanship and reported in writing within 60 calendar days after substantial completion. The company receives an opportunity to inspect and, if covered, correct the work. Product failures, normal wear, abuse, moisture, pests, movement, customer changes, and third-party work are excluded.

## Schedule and payment

Dates are good-faith estimates unless guaranteed in writing. Weather, shortages, inspections, permits, changes, hidden conditions, illness, and unsafe conditions can extend the schedule.

The customer follows the written deposit and payment schedule. Available app choices are Cash App, cash, card, check, or bank transfer. The final receipt records the method and payment reference when provided.

## Photos and privacy

Photos may document conditions, work, estimates, warranties, insurance matters, and disputes. Identifying customer photos should not be used in public advertising without separate permission.

## Disputes

The customer should give written details of a concern and a reasonable opportunity to inspect and correct covered work before filing a claim, except when emergency action is reasonably necessary.

## Ohio cancellation and estimate rights

Some transactions signed at a customer’s residence may be Ohio home solicitation sales. Those transactions can require a written agreement, oral notice, detachable cancellation forms, and a three-business-day cancellation period. Residential repair or service work can also trigger estimate-choice disclosures. The app flags these issues but does not decide whether an exception applies to a particular job.

## Review with an attorney

- Registration and use of the Niko Residential Holdings operating name
- Exact cancellation notice and when it is required
- Local contractor registration or licensing
- Permit and insurance responsibility
- Deposits and progress payments
- Mechanic’s-lien notices
- Late fees and collection costs
- Worker versus independent-contractor classification
- Warranty wording and lawful liability limits
